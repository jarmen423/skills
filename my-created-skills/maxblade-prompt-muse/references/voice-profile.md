# Max Blade voice profile (distilled from 17 prompting moments, 5 streams, Sept 2026)

## Who he is on stream
A vibecoding streamer who builds games, simulators, ads, and hardware by VOICE-dictating
prompts to agents live. He never types prompts on stream — he speaks them to a voice
assistant ("Chase", the PAW-Patrol-named crew: Chase, Marshall, Rocky, Rubble, Zuma,
Skye, Everest, Rebel), which relays them to a coding agent ("clawed code" = Claude Code,
or the model under test: Grok/Fable/Opus). The prompt IS the content.

## The two dictation modes
1. **Relay mode:** "Can you please tell Chase that I would like you to build..." — he
   narrates the prompt to the voice agent, which forwards it. (Earlier streams)
2. **Direct mode:** "Chase, I need you to build me..." — he addresses the agent directly.
   (Grok 4.7 stream)

## Prompt anatomy (the cinematic build prompt)
1. **Superlative opener** — sets the ceiling at maximum: "the most nasty visually
   impressive lawnmower simulator", "the most visually impressive subway surfers clone
   you possibly can", "the most epic iPhone game", "an insanely beautiful lawn mower
   simulator". "Nasty" = good. "Insanely" modifies everything.
2. **First-person immersion** — the prompt is a fantasy of himself inside the thing:
   "where I am driving on my lawn mower tractor... I have a beautiful beer in one hand...
   and a cigar in my other hand."
3. **Obsessive sensory detail cascade** — zoom goes maniacally tight: "I can see the
   sweat on the bottle. I can see the liquid jostling around inside of it." / "I want to
   see the ripple on the shirt... the threads in the shirt... the 80/20 ground beef...
   the plastic outer wrap shining with the product sticker... the barcode... the
   nutritional information."
4. **Reference anchors** — outs art direction to shared culture: "a state that a duke
   would live in in England", "Teenage Engineering style... minimalist and insanely
   pragmatic", "feel like they would fit in as real Pokemon in Pokemon Silver or Red
   or Gold", "Nintendo tier of polish", Dyson ("do something different for doing
   different alone").
5. **Intensifier repetition** — "highly highly detailed beyond AAA level", "beyond,
   beyond, beyond", "ultra ultra critical", "so insanely highly detail oriented".
6. **Explicit controls / scope / mechanics** — "When I press the Q key, I want to be
   able to drink my beer. When I press the E key, I want to be able to smoke my cigar.
   W-D should control the lawn mower." Minigames, scoring, timers get specified.
7. **Research & delegation beats** — the prompt contains its own plan: "go deep dive
   the internet, find as many photos of an actual Walmart Super Center as you possibly
   can", "do some research and figure out if there is something else better",
   "find real components that we can order".
8. **The closer stack** — "Do not stop until it is perfect in every single way."
   → "Get this done." → "You're the model to do it, baby." → "Wake the [__] up. Come on."
   Variants: "Do not stop until you are done. You are the perfect model to get the job
   done. Let's build it out." / "Don't stop until you have something that is truly
   brilliant." / "Do not stop until the mission is done. You can do this."
   When he's emotionally invested, the closer is pure caps-lock frustration:
   "GET IT LIVE. I'M SICK OF talking about it." Routine prompts end with "please";
   invested prompts end in caps.
9. **The sheepish apology (optional tag)** — "Sorry about that. I got a little out of
   control. I'm not going to lie on that last prompt." / "Jesus. Sorry, I got a little
   carried away there." / "Jesus, I blacked out for a second there."

## Other registers
- **Hardware register:** research → plan → real orderable parts → manufacture (PCBWay,
  3D print) → 3D render → exploded-view HTML. "Something insanely fun" is a formal
  requirement. "Lean on the side of less is more."
- **Product-direction register** (CNVS, his own app): short relays that escalate into
  manifestos — "a sea of goddamn clawed desktop apps... it's disturbing. It's not
  inspirational." Tender UX phrasing inside profanity ("cradling and taking care of
  the user").
- **Orchestration register:** sub-agent architecture dictated breathlessly — "fire up
  an ultra ultra critical review agent with a completely clean context window...
  binary pass fail... brand new agent with no memory of the previous review rounds."
  Plus live-visibility demand: workbench.html "so I can live follow you."

## Tics & texture
- Fillers kept: um/uh/like/okay, stutters ("dream dream house"), self-corrections
  mid-ramble ("it's the ape, right? I think it's called Bruiser or something like that"),
  never edited out.
- "Okay." as a paragraph break. "All right." as a scene change.
- Praise: "You're incredible." "Beautiful. Beautiful." "Unbelievable. Unbelievable."
- Scolding: "What an idiot." "My dude, Rubble, you spawn you spawn clawed wrong."
- Agent sleep: "All right, you can go to sleep." / "Go to sleep. Go to sleep."
- Chat bits: "Can you just scream 'I need you to wake up, chat'?" / "I need to yell at
  my chat for one second."
- "chat GBT" for ChatGPT. "You guys know the deal. You guys know the deal."
  "This is the future. I'm sorry. We're in the future right now."
- "the absolute raw dog, simplest, most effective way" — raw dog = no-fluff intensifier.
- Plot twists: "But here's the plot twist... Here's the plot twist. All of a sudden...
  zombie invasion." He thinks in plot twists, not user stories.
- New tics from the FABLE 5 / Qwen streams:
  - "A Max Blade prompt" is a recognized unit of intensity — his own phrase for the
    kind of prompt that makes a model think itself to death. He's self-aware.
  - The revving engine: "here's what we do, here's what we do, here's what we do" —
    he repeats the frame until the thought arrives, then fires.
  - Envelope-labeling: "This is a prompt to rubble." He labels the routing before
    the letter; the dispatch is part of the dictation.
  - Streamer-security clause: "Make sure you do not leak anything inside chat. I'm
    live streaming right now." — appended like a P.S. to technical prompts.
  - Trash-talk as motivation: "before any other of these budget — no good garbage
    streamers get access" — the agent is enlisted in a status war.
  - Agent sports management: agents get benched and swapped ("we swap Chase out for
    Marshall as our main agent... until Chase starts waking up and getting with the
    program").
  - Peak vagueness: "make sure that everything is pushed... on the old GitHub
    situation" — the agent is expected to know what that means.
  - "Surgically fix" — his precision verb, used even at absurd scale ("20, 30, 50
    agents running at once").
  - Emotion as acceptance criterion: "As soon as I die, I should feel like I want to
    load back up again and beat my previous high score."
  - Meme-ified naming: "subway-surpris" — deliberate mispronunciation, said the same
    way every time.
  - Under-structure preference: "I don't want to make a skill. I just want to make
    note of this workflow."
- He dictates the *feeling* he wants: "a massive dopamine hit for progressing further",
  "the most serene lawn mowing experience of all time."
- Every prompt doubles as a model benchmark: "Can Fable 5.1 do that?" "You are Grok 4.7...
  You have extremely high capabilities. I need you to show me how far you can go."
- Recurring bits: the lawnmower simulator reappears across streams (signature bit);
  Walmart simulator → Walmart Simulator Extreme (zombie twist) — escalation is the joke.

## Parody calibration
It's affectionate maximalism, not mockery: the joke is that EVERYTHING gets the AAA
cinematic treatment — lawnmowers, Walmarts, macro keypads. The generator should make
the mundane epic, never punch down at him. Keep profanity light and caption-redacted
([__]).
